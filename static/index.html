      How SafeNet decides

      SafeNet does not guess. It looks each piece up in its database and adds up what it finds.

      Look it up

      The link, sender and message are compared with known phishing links, blocked senders, trusted websites and known scam messages.

      Check for warning signs

      Scam wording, look-alike brand names, odd website endings and links that do not match the brand each add to the risk.

      Add it up and advise

      The evidence is combined into a 0 to 100 score with the exact reasons, and SafeNet says what to do next.

      Habits that stop most scams

      No tool is perfect. These rules protect you even when a message looks convincing.

      Never share codes or passwordsBanks and delivery companies do not ask for your OTP, PIN or password by message or call.
      Read the real domainThe part just before the first single slash decides where you land. paypal.com.login-help.xyz is not PayPal.
      Treat urgency as a warning"Within 24 hours" and "account blocked" are designed to stop you thinking.
      Open the app yourselfType the address or use the official app instead of tapping a link in a message.
      Verify through another channelCall the number printed on your card or the company's own website.
      Report and deleteTell your bank or your country's cybercrime portal so others are protected.

      What SafeNet checks against

      Every result comes from matches in these records, not from guesses. When a new scam is reported it is added to the database and caught from then on.

      SafeNet is a decision-support prototype. It does not replace your bank's or employer's security tools. Each check (your message, link and the result) is saved in the SafeNet database so it can be reviewed later, so do not paste passwords or private details.
